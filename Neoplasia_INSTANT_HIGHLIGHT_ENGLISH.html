Definition

🎯 Golden Sheet — أهم ما يجب حفظه

  • Neoplasm: abnormal, excessive, uncoordinated growth persisting after cessation of stimulus.
  • Benign: localized, no metastasis. Borderline: local invasion, no metastasis. Malignant: invasion, destruction, metastasis possible.
  • Parenchyma = proliferating neoplastic cells; stroma = connective tissue + blood vessels.
  • Desmoplasia = abundant collagenous stroma → hard tumor; example: scirrhous carcinoma breast.
  • −oma is not always benign: lymphoma, melanoma, glioma, seminoma.
  • Benign tumors = well differentiated. Malignant tumors = variable differentiation; anaplasia may occur.
  • Abnormal mitoses strongly support malignancy; mitosis alone is not diagnostic.
  • Benign growth = cohesive + expansile; malignant growth = infiltrative + invasive + destructive.
  • Metastasis routes = lymphatic, hematogenous, and seeding of body cavities.
  • Sentinel lymph node = first regional node receiving lymph drainage from primary tumor.
  • Portal venous drainage → liver; caval venous drainage → lungs.
  • Dysplasia = non-neoplastic, premalignant, disordered epithelial growth; may be reversible.
  • CIS = full-thickness atypia + intact basement membrane; invasive carcinoma breaches basement membrane.

✍️ Short Essay — أسئلة تدريبية مرشّحة

★★★ Benign vs borderline malignant vs malignant tumors
  • Benign: localized, no metastasis, usually excisable.
  • Borderline: localized, invades surrounding tissue, metastasis absent (according to supplied notes).
  • Malignant: invasion, destruction of adjacent tissues, distant metastasis may occur.
★★★ Carcinoma vs sarcoma
  • Carcinoma = malignant epithelial tumor.
  • Sarcoma = malignant mesenchymal tumor.
★★★ Pathways of metastasis
  • Lymphatic spread.
  • Hematogenous spread.
  • Seeding body cavities.
★★★ Dysplasia vs CIS vs invasive carcinoma
  • Dysplasia: disordered epithelial growth; basement membrane intact; may be reversible.
  • CIS: full-thickness epithelial atypia; basement membrane intact; no invasion.
  • Invasive carcinoma: basement membrane breached; underlying tissue invaded.
★★ Differentiation and anaplasia
  • Differentiation: morphological and functional similarity to normal cells.
  • Anaplasia: loss of differentiation with primitive cellular appearance.
  • Benign tumors are well differentiated; malignant tumors show variable differentiation.
